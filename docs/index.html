ACD-GS yields sparser Gaussians while preserving scene structure, and sits in the most favorable storage–time–quality region.

Abstract

Geometry-aware densification at its source

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for real-time photorealistic novel view synthesis. However, its training-time densification remains largely heuristic, often resulting in redundant Gaussian proliferation and inaccurate geometric reconstruction. Existing methods predominantly rely on first-order screen-space gradients to trigger cloning and splitting, which fail to distinguish true geometric discontinuities from high-frequency textures or noisy residuals. To address this limitation, we propose ACD-GS, an Asymmetric Curvature-aware Densification framework for compact and geometry-aware 3D Gaussian optimization. Our key insight is that second-order depth responses provide a more reliable structural prior for density control, enabling asymmetric regulation of cloning and splitting to better capture geometric discontinuities. Building on this, we introduce a multi-view photometric consistency constraint to suppress transient single-view errors, together with a capacity-aware adaptive regulation strategy that balances densification and pruning during training. Unlike post-hoc compression or quantization-based approaches, ACD-GS directly prevents redundant Gaussian generation during training, yielding inherently compact representations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method reduces nearly 60% of Gaussian count and storage while maintaining competitive rendering fidelity, consistently outperforming existing methods across multiple datasets.

58.6% storage reduction on Deep Blending (367 MB → 152 MB)
~60% fewer Gaussians than 3DGS across all benchmarks (1.55M → 642K)
1.5–2× faster training (e.g., 683 s → 345 s on Mip-NeRF 360)
+1.1 dB PSNR over 3DGS on Deep Blending (24.03 → 25.12, best PSNR/SSIM)

Method

From first-order gradients to second-order geometry priors

First-order screen-space gradients conflate flat textured surfaces, smooth slopes, and true depth discontinuities. ACD-GS instead reads the curvature of the rendered depth to build an edge-confidence map, then uses it to decouple cloning and splitting — suppressing redundant primitives at their source rather than compressing them after training.

1

Curvature-Guided Asymmetric Densification

A depth-edge confidence map E is obtained from the clipped and normalized second-order depth response. Each visible Gaussian samples E at its projected center, yielding ei, which modulates its cloning and splitting gradients:

giclone=gi(1−αei),gisplit=gi(1+βei).

Cloning is suppressed near depth discontinuities to reduce redundant duplication, while splitting is promoted to refine boundary-crossing Gaussians. In smooth regions, the original gradient is preserved.

2

Multi-view Photometric Error Gating

To filter transient photometric errors, K=8 auxiliary views are sampled during densification. Each Gaussian is scored by the fraction of valid views in which its projection falls within a high-RGB-error region:

Sirgb=∑k=1Kvi,kei,krgbmax(∑k=1Kvi,k,1).

A Gaussian is admitted into the densification candidate pool if

Sirgb≥τmv.

This cross-view constraint filters view-specific noise while preserving genuinely under-reconstructed details.

3

Capacity-Constrained Adaptive Optimization

A scene-aware growth quota determines the number of eligible densification candidates:

Nallow=⌊μNtotal⌋,μ=0.8%.

The gradient threshold is adaptively selected from the sorted gradient distribution, with a top-10% fallback when the candidate pool is smaller than the quota.

Opacity pruning is progressively strengthened during training:

αprune(p)=αmin+(αmax−αmin)p,p=tT.

This preserves potentially useful Gaussians during early optimization while removing redundant and low-opacity primitives toward convergence.
